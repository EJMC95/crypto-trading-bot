## What I fixed this run — `(mb)`

**This review had been overwriting its own previous report every single
morning, and I found it by destroying one.**

The filename came from `reviewed_at[:10]`, a **UTC** date. This job runs at
08:00 Sydney = **22:00 UTC the previous day**, so today's run wrote
`evidence_review_2026-08-13.md` — over yesterday's finished 31,223-byte report,
human layer included, replaced by 6,674 bytes of fresh output. Not a race:
every Sydney time from 10:00 to midnight maps to the previous UTC day, so the
collision was structural and had been happening for as long as the job has run
in the morning slot.

**The selftest was pinning the bug.** Its one fixture,
`2026-08-05T21:46:47+00:00`, is 07:46 the next morning in Sydney — the cron's
own slot — and it asserted the collision was *correct*, with a comment
defending it. That is why nothing caught this.

Shipped: `report_day()` keys the series on the **Sydney** date (fleet internals
stay UTC — `reviewed_at` and every ledger join are untouched), and
`preserve_existing_report()` renames an existing report aside and **names the
backup in the new report's body**, so a same-day re-run cannot silently eat
your annotations. Both fail open. **Mutation-verified ×5, all red.**

Yesterday's report is not recoverable (`reports/` is not committed). This
morning's misfiled copy is kept as
`evidence_review_2026-08-13.MISFILED-was-14aug-run.md` rather than deleted, so
the record of what happened survives.
