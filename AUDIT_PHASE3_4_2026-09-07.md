# PHASE 3 — RESEARCH QUALITY

## 3.0 The headline: survivorship, and it is large

Every grading instrument in this fleet scores the **living** set —
`golive_readiness.roster` admits publishers, `fleet_allocation` ranks living
rows, `edge_audit` audits the published grade. Retirement is decided per book on
a measured exclusion (I17), which is correct. The aggregate consequence had
never been computed:

| population (Lighter era, since the 17-Jul cut) | books | closes | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| still publishing | 16 | 1,984 | **+$296.09** |
| retired since the cut | 18 | 1,549 | **−$260.47** |
| **TRUE FLEET TOTAL** | 34 | 3,533 | **+$35.63** |

**The living-book figure overstates the fleet's realised result by 8.3×.**

This reopens no retirement. It says something narrower and harder: a loser
leaves the sample the day it is retired, so *the number that survives is not the
number that was earned*, and both belong in the record. Now computed every run
by `baseline_snapshot.survivorship()`, scoped to the Lighter era on purpose —
three pre-cut legacy books (+$547 combined, other venues) would bias it the
other way.

## 4.1 The measured risk finding — beta, and why the obvious read is wrong

Using the regime index over each trade's own holding window:

| weighting | fleet beta | reading |
|---|---|---|
| **trade-weighted** | **+0.04** | looks market-neutral |
| **exposure-weighted** | **+0.324** | is not |
| **LIVE cohort only** | **+0.658** | real money is substantially long the market |

Trade-weighting is an artifact: 🪁 kelly contributes **590 of 1,740** labelled
trades at tiny clips and a beta of **−0.76**, so it dominates the count and
almost nothing of the risk. Exposure-weighting is the correct lens.

And the composition matters more than the number:

* **profitable books: mean beta +0.40** (georgia +0.99, taker +0.80,
  georgia-v3 +0.75, mum live +0.66)
* **loss-making books: mean beta −0.12** (kelly −0.76, sniper −0.58,
  Counterweight −0.25)

**The fleet earns from long beta and pays for its hedge.** Its market-neutrality
is supplied by books that lose money — kelly alone is −$132.53. That is a
portfolio-construction problem, not a per-book one, and no per-book instrument
can see it.

Two supporting reads from the same data:

* **🌾 carry carries statistically significant long beta (+0.12, t=3.26)** on a
  book whose P&L is defined as `accrued − fees` with **no price term**. A
  delta-neutral book should not have a beta. Worth a look before it is scaled.
* **Every profitable directional book loses money when the market falls**
  (mum live +0.878% up / **−0.442% down**; georgia +0.881% / **−0.675%**;
  taker +1.898% / +0.185%). The one exception is 🙏 avo (+2.865% / +1.097%),
  on n=14.

## Rollback procedure

Nothing is deployed, so rollback is currently trivial — but stated in full for
whatever ships next:

1. **This audit:** `git revert` the audit commits, or simply close PR #290. No
   container has taken anything; no lever, env or position was touched.
2. **Verify the protected surface:**
   `python3 audit_fingerprint.py --ledger <trades.json> --state fingerprint.json --check`
   → must read `5/5 protected symbols unchanged`, `0 rewritten`.
3. **For any future module:** every fleet change here is reversible by a named
   env var (the `*_RETIRED_OVERRIDE` / `*_MODE=advisory` convention). A module
   must ship with one, or it is not deployable.
4. **Live services:** revert the commit, then re-deploy with the marker in
   **both** the PR title and the commit subject (a squash merge drops branch
   subjects — measured on `(xe)`, which never reached 🙏 avo's live arm).
5. **Verify the revert landed** by `extra.build` + `extra.build_n` on the row,
   never by a green CI run.
